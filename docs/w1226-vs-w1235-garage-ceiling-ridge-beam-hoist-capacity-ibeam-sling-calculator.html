Frequently Asked Questions (W12×26 vs W12×35 Garage Ceiling Ridge Beam Hoist Capacity)

What size steel I-beam do I need for W12×26 vs W12×35 Garage Ceiling Ridge Beam Hoist Capacity (4,000 lbs on a 18-ft span)?

Including a 15% dynamic hoist impact factor (4,720 lbs design load), keeping mid-span deflection below the CMAA $L/600$ limit (0.36 inches) over a 18-foot span requires an AISC W12×35 Wide-Flange beam ($I_x = 285.0\text{ in}^4$, 6.56" flange width), which deflects only 0.12 inches at full load.

How does rigging sling angle affect chain tension when lifting 4,000 lbs?

Per ASME B30.9, tension per leg equals $\text{Load} \div (N \times \sin\theta)$. On a 2-leg sling lifting 4,000 lbs: at 90° vertical each leg holds 2,000 lbs; at 60° each leg holds 2,309 lbs (1.15x); at 45° each leg holds 2,828 lbs (1.41x); and at 30° each leg holds 4,000 lbs (2.0x).

What is the difference between a W-Beam (W12×35 Wide-Flange) and an S-Beam for a hoist trolley?

Wide-Flange (W-Shapes) have wider, parallel flat flanges (6.56" wide) with higher lateral stability over long spans, whereas American Standard I-Beams (S-Shapes) have narrower, 16.7% sloped inner flanges crafted specifically for traditional tapered trolley wheels. Universal dual-tread trolleys ride smoothly on both.
